# Visual Direction

## Surface Mode

Define visitor success before composing:

- **Persuade**: understand a proposition, decide, and act.
- **Operate**: complete a task predictably and efficiently.
- **Read**: understand structured information.
- **Experience**: enter a body of work, portfolio, or artwork; the artifact leads and the interface recedes.

The mode belongs to the surface, not the whole product.

## Kind of Work

- **Creation**: no existing system constrains the direction.
- **Redesign**: content and function remain; the old render becomes evidence and an anti-reference.
- **Refinement**: identity, behavior, and out-of-scope content remain unchanged; the deliverable is a precise delta.

## Direction to Define

- palette: four to six colors named by role;
- typography: roles, scale, weights, and rhythm;
- composition: one-sentence concept, hierarchy, grid, and adaptation;
- signature: the memorable element tied to the subject;
- imagery and brand: source, treatment, constraints, and role.

Review every choice: if it could have been produced unchanged for any neighboring subject, it is not yet a direction. Decorative labels, numbering, dividers, effects, and motion must encode information or disappear.

Avoid automatic recipes—purple gradients, card accumulation, unjustified trendy fonts, bouncy animation, generic editorial palettes—unless the brief makes them relevant. A minimalist direction demands precision; an expressive direction requires enough execution depth to fulfill its promise.
